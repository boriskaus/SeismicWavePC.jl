Curvilinear solver: method and related work

The curvilinear solver was written for SeismicWavePC; unlike the staggered-grid solver it is not a port of an existing code. It follows no single paper. It combines established ingredients of summation-by-parts (SBP) finite differences with simultaneous approximation terms (SAT); this page lists them and relates the solver to published SBP-SAT methods for seismic waves. How to use the solver: Topography on a curvilinear grid.

The scheme

  • Equations. The first-order velocity–stress form of the visco-elastic wave equation (generalized Zener body and VTI anisotropy as in the staggered solver), transformed to a terrain-following mesh with vertical columns: x = ξ (and y = ψ), z = Z(ξ, η). With J = ∂Z/∂η and Jx = −∂Z/∂ξ the momentum equation is ρJ ∂t vᵢ = Dξ(J σᵢₓ) + Dη(Jx σᵢₓ + σᵢ_z), and the strain rates use ∂x = Dξ + (Jx/J) Dη, ∂z = Dη / J (3D: the same with ψ and Jy).
  • SBP operators. Upwind dual-pairing operators D₊, D₋ with H D₊ + (H D₋)ᵀ = B (Mattsson, 2017), with the boundary-optimized closures of Mattsson, Niemelä & Winters (2027), order 7, from SummationByPartsOperators.jl (Ranocha, 2021). The momentum equation uses D₋, the strain rates D₊; this pairing gives a discrete energy estimate, as for the dual-pairing elastic solvers of Duru, Fung & Williams (2022). In tests on a hill model the boundary-optimized closures gave the smallest errors near the free surface among the operators compared (Mattsson, 2017, orders 5 and 7; the dispersion-relation-preserving operators of Williams & Duru, 2024); central SBP operators (Mattsson & Nordström, 2004) let point sources excite spurious grid-scale modes.
  • Boundaries and interfaces (SAT). Boundary and interface conditions are imposed weakly with penalty terms (Carpenter, Gottlieb & Abarbanel, 1994; reviews: Svärd & Nordström, 2014; Del Rey Fernández, Hicken & Zingg, 2014). The free surface removes the traction. The absorbing sides and bottom use impedance (characteristic) conditions, integrated implicitly (trapezoidal rule), so they do not limit the time step. Material interfaces are grid lines between blocks: half the traction mismatch enters the momentum equations and half the velocity mismatch the strain rates of both sides (welded). At fluid–solid interfaces only the normal velocity and normal traction are coupled and the tangential tractions are removed (slip); Lotto & Dunham (2015) couple a compressible ocean to an elastic solid with SBP-SAT as well.
  • Stability and time stepping. With these terms the semi-discrete energy E = ½ Σ H (ρJ |v|² + J σ:C⁻¹σ) is conserved (or dissipated at the absorbing boundaries). The time integration is a leapfrog scheme; its time step follows from the spectral radius of the spatial operator (power iteration).
  • Absorbing layers. absorber = :pml applies the complex-frequency-shifted PML of OpenSWPC (auxiliary differential equations, Zhang & Shen, 2010) to the ξ and η parts of the SBP operators. absorber = :mpml is the multiaxial PML (Meza-Fajardo & Papageorgiou, 2008) in its complex-frequency-shifted form, as formulated by Zhang, Zhang & Chen (2014) for elastic waves on curvilinear grids. Unlike the interior scheme, the layers are not covered by an energy estimate; for provably stable PMLs with SBP-SAT see Duru & Kreiss (2014) and Duru, Kozdon & Kreiss (2015).
  • Sources and stations. Point sources and receivers use a discrete delta: a Kaiser-windowed sinc (Hicks, 2002) where the grid is uniform around the point, Lagrange weights near boundaries; stations with surface = true sit exactly on the topography.
  • Gradients. The SBP identity H A = −(H J G)ᵀ between the momentum operator A and the strain-rate operator G (SAT terms included) gives the exact discrete adjoint by applying the forward operators, so misfit gradients and Born modelling are exact for the discrete scheme. Wiratama, Duru & Kim (2026) develop a space-time dual-pairing SBP framework for forward and adjoint wave equations.

Related work

SBP-SAT methods for seismic waves on curvilinear, boundary-conforming meshes have a substantial literature:

  • Duru & Dunham (2016) solve the first-order elastic wave equation with SBP operators on curvilinear meshes, with weakly imposed boundary and interface conditions and semi-discrete energy estimates, for dynamic ruptures on nonplanar faults.
  • Duru, Fung & Williams (2022) use dual-pairing (upwind) SBP operators for large-scale elastic wave simulations in 3D complex geometries, the closest relative of the operators used here; Duru, Williams & Fung (2022, preprint) add dispersion-relation-preserving operators that avoid spurious high-frequency modes, for nonlinear ruptures.
  • Petersson & Sjögreen (2015) discretize the anisotropic elastic wave equation in second-order form on curvilinear grids with SBP operators (the code SW4).
  • Almquist & Dunham (2021) treat the general anisotropic elastic wave equation on curvilinear multiblock grids in second-order form, with weakly enforced boundary and interface conditions.
  • Kozdon, Dunham & Nordström (2012) impose boundary and (frictional) interface conditions for elastodynamics weakly with SBP operators, and show the benefit of characteristic variables.
  • Lotto & Dunham (2015) couple a compressible ocean to an elastic solid with SBP-SAT.
  • Duru & Kreiss (2014) and Duru, Kozdon & Kreiss (2015) analyze the stability of PMLs for the elastic wave equation, with SBP-SAT discretizations.
  • O'Reilly et al. (2022) use SBP operators on staggered curvilinear grids with topography (4th order, provably stable), for the GPU code AWP-ODC.
  • de la Puente et al. (2014) take a different route: a fully staggered (Lebedev) grid deformed to the topography, with mimetic operators at the free surface (no energy estimate; 4× the memory of a standard staggered grid). Their test, Garvin's problem on tilted surfaces, is in Validation.

The accuracy of the curvilinear solver on topography, against the staggered grid, is shown in Validation (free surface on topography).
