Misfit gradients

using SeismicWavePC, GeophysicalModelGenerator
observed = simulate(true_model; dt, nt, sources=source, stations)      # or recorded data, as Seismograms
J, grad, sgrad = misfit_gradient(start_model, observed; dt, nt, sources=source)
grad.fields.vs                                                          # ∂J/∂vs on the grid of the model (per km/s)
sgrad[1].M, sgrad[1].t0                                                 # ∂J/∂(moment tensor), ∂J/∂(origin time)

  • Misfit: misfit=L2Misfit() (default) is J = ½ Σ (v - v_observed)² over all samples, components and stations of observed, whose stations are used.

    • L2Misfit(weights=w, window=(t1, t2)) weights stations, components or samples and selects time windows (one for all, or one per station); L2Misfit(invcov=C⁻¹) uses an inverse covariance matrix of the samples of a trace (correlated noise); L2Misfit(normalize=true) divides the misfit of each trace by the energy of the observed trace.
    • TravelTimeMisfit(; weights, window) uses the cross-correlation time shifts of the traces.
    • EnvelopeMisfit() compares the envelopes (insensitive to the phase, robust against cycle skipping), PhaseMisfit() the instantaneous phases (insensitive to amplitudes), TFPhaseMisfit(width=T) the phase differences in time and frequency (Fichtner et al., 2008).
    • Filtered(misfit, (f1, f2)) applies a misfit to band-pass filtered synthetic and observed seismograms (zero-phase Butterworth, bandpass), for inversions from low to high frequencies.
    • geographic_weights(stations, L) gives station weights that balance clusters of stations.
    • Any function (syn, obs, t) -> (J, ∂J/∂syn) of the synthetic and observed velocities [it, component, station] works too.

  • Several shots: misfit_gradient(model, [obs1, obs2, …]; sources=[src1, src2, …], …) sums the misfits and model gradients of the shots; each shot can have several sources. misfit is one misfit for all shots, or a vector with one per shot (e.g. event weights).

  • Model gradient: grad is a CartData with the fields rho, vp, vs, Qp, Qs. With mute=(sources=r, stations=r) it is tapered to zero within the distance r [km] of the sources and stations, where it is large and of little use.

  • Teleseismic waves: with a TeleseismicWave in sources the gradient is exact as well (the injection along the box acts as sources that do not depend on the model inside it; its dependence on the material at the box boundary is included).

  • Illumination: with illumination=true, grad also has the field illumination: Σₜ |v|² of the source wavefield [(m/s)²] per cell (summed over the shots, not muted). Dividing the gradient by it (clipped near the sources, plus a small constant) is a simple preconditioner (the diagonal of a pseudo-Hessian) for inversions and compensates the illumination in reverse-time migration (Examples).

  • Source gradients: sgrad has one entry per source (per shot: a vector):

    • moment tensors: M (∂J/∂ of the six components M0 .* M) and M0;
    • forces: F (∂J/∂ of fx, fy, fz);
    • both: the location x, y, z, t0, trise, and stf, the gradient with respect to the source time function at the times t. With a sampled source time function (stf = SampledSTF(t, values)), the gradient with respect to its samples follows from stf by the chain rule of the linear interpolation.

    With interpolation = :sinc (sources between grid points, see Sources and stations) all source gradients are exact, including the location. With the default interpolation = :cell the source sits at a cell, and the location gradient comes from the spatial derivative of the adjoint field (an approximation).

  • All keywords of simulate work: GPUs (backend), MPI (nproc; every process gets all results), T, absorber, record_every, stf, ….

  • Method: adjoint-state method with checkpointing. The forward wavefield is saved every few steps (balancing host and device memory) and the steps in between are recomputed; the adjoint simulation runs backwards.

  • Exactness: the adjoint kernels are the exact transposes of the forward kernels. They include the 2nd-order cells at free surfaces and the PML or M-PML (with adjoint PML memory variables), and adjoint memory variables of attenuation give the Qp, Qs gradients. The result agrees with reverse-mode automatic differentiation to round-off (10⁻¹⁵ with T = Float64) in 2D and 3D. It is approximate only for the Cerjan sponge, the side extrapolation of plane waves, and the density in the source cell of P-SV body forces.

  • Cost: about 5 forward simulations. On Marmousi2 with 641 receivers a gradient takes 80 s on 12 CPU threads and 30 s on an Apple GPU, 5× and 13× faster than SeismicWaves.jl (Performance).

  • method=:ad (with using Enzyme) differentiates the whole simulation automatically instead: exact in all cases, but 20–50 forward simulations, on the CPU and one process, with the default misfit and without source gradients.

See misfit_gradient and the misfits (L2Misfit, TravelTimeMisfit, EnvelopeMisfit, PhaseMisfit, TFPhaseMisfit, Filtered) for all options.

Born modelling (the Jacobian)

born computes the seismograms of a model perturbation δm to first order, J δm (J: the Jacobian of the seismograms with respect to the model), together with the seismograms of the model itself, in one simulation:

δm = CartData(model.x.val, model.y.val, model.z.val, (; vp=0.1 .* blob))   # any of rho, vp, vs, Qp, Qs
background, scattered = born(model, δm; dt, nt, sources, stations)
  • Exactness: it is the exact linearization of the discrete simulation (free surface, PML, attenuation), and the transpose of the gradient of misfit_gradient: ⟨J δm, r⟩ = ⟨δm, Jᵀ r⟩ to rounding errors (10⁻¹⁵ with T = Float64), for P-SV, SH and 3D, moment tensors and forces, at cells or between them.
  • δm may also perturb the Thomsen parameters epsilon, delta, gamma of VTI models.
  • Use: with misfit_gradient (Jᵀ r) it gives Gauss-Newton Hessian-vector products JᵀJ δm (Newton-type inversions, uncertainties of the Laplace approximation) and least-squares migration; alone, it shows how a model change shows up in the data.
  • How: the simulation runs with numbers that carry a tangent (forward-mode differentiation through the kernels): the medium holds the perturbation, and the tangent of the wavefield is the scattered wavefield.
  • Cost: about 2.5 forward simulations on GPUs, 4.5 on CPU threads. All keywords of simulate work, including MPI (nproc), except teleseismic waves (for now).
