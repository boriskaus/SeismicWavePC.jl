Sources and stations

Sources

  • DoubleCoupleSource(x, y, z; Mw (or M0), strike, dip, rake, t0, trise): a double couple from fault angles (strike clockwise from north = +y).
  • MomentTensorSource(x, y, z, M0, M; t0, trise): a general moment tensor M = (xx, yy, zz, yz, xz, xy) in the model frame.
  • ForceSource(x, y, z; fx, fy, fz, t0, trise): a point force [N] (OpenSWPC's body force).
  • ExplosionSource(x, y, z; M0 or Mw, t0, trise): an explosion (isotropic moment tensor, diagonal components M0).
  • TeleseismicWave(:p, :sv or :sh; angle, backazimuth, box, t0, trise, amplitude): a plane wave from below, injected along a box (see Teleseismic waves).
  • PlaneWave(:p or :s; depth, length, strike, dip, rake): an incoming plane wave as an initial wavefield (OpenSWPC's pw_mode; for teleseismic modelling use TeleseismicWave).

All can be combined in a vector, and all accept (lon, lat, z, proj::ProjectionPoint) instead of x, y, z. Sources outside the model or in the air are ignored with a warning (as in OpenSWPC, e.g. parts of a finite fault).

Finite faults

A kinematic rupture is a set of point sources, the subfaults, each with its own slip, rake, rupture time, rise time and source time function. finite_fault makes them for a planar fault:

slip(a, b) = 2.0 * exp(-(a^2 / 30 + (b - 6)^2 / 10))           # [m]; a along strike from the centre, b down dip [km]
fault = finite_fault(; top=(0.0, 0.0, -2.0), strike=20, dip=60, length=16.0, width=12.0, nl=50, nw=40,
                     slip, rake=90, model, hypocenter=(-4.0, 8.0), vr=2.8, trise=1.0)
result = simulate(model; tmax=30.0, sources=fault, stations)

The moment of a subfault is μ A slip, with μ = ρ vs² of the model at the subfault (or a given mu). Slip, rake and rise time can also be matrices (e.g. from a published slip model), and the rupture times can be given instead of a rupture velocity. In 2D models the fault is a line in the x–z plane (moments per unit length). Faults with thousands of subfaults run on GPUs as well: the amplitudes of all sources at all time steps are kept on the device in windows of time steps when they would not fit as a whole (and the source sensitivities of misfit_gradient likewise), with the same results. Misfit gradients with respect to every subfault (moment tensor, location, timing, source time function) come with the gradient, for kinematic source inversions.

Source files.

  • read_srf: kinematic ruptures in the Standard Rupture Format (SRF, versions 1 and 2), e.g. of the SCEC Broadband Platform: one subfault per point, with its slip-velocity function, rupture time and moment (μ from the file or the model).
  • read_cmtsolution: CMTSOLUTION files (SPECFEM, Global CMT; several blocks for finite sources), with SPECFEM's Gaussian moment rate of the given half duration.
  • read_sources: OpenSWPC's source lists (fn_stf, every stf_format: xym0ij, xym0dc, llm0dc, …, xydsdc, psmeca, and the body forces of bf_mode): one point source per line, OpenSWPC's way to describe finite faults.

Teleseismic waves

TeleseismicWave models a plane wave arriving from below at an angle of incidence and a backazimuth, as from a distant earthquake: receiver functions, teleseismic tomography and full-waveform inversion of teleseismic body waves.

w = TeleseismicWave(:p; angle=20, backazimuth=240, box=(5, 35, 5, 35, -30), trise=1.0, t0=0.5)   # 3D; 2D: box=(xmin, xmax, zmin)
r = simulate(model; dt, nt, sources=w, stations)

The wave is injected along the sides and the bottom of box (total-field / scattered-field method): inside the box the simulation computes the total field (the incoming wave, its reflections at the free surface and at the layer interfaces, and the waves scattered by the structure in the box), outside only the scattered waves, which leave through the absorbing boundaries. The incoming field is the exact solution for the horizontally layered background in which the box sits (computed in the frequency domain for every depth of the grid, with all reverberations), so

  • the wave enters the box continuously from the bottom and the sides, at any angle and backazimuth, without artefacts from the edges of the model;
  • P, SV and SH waves, in 2D (P-SV, SH) and 3D, on CPUs, GPUs and with MPI, also on grids stretched in depth;
  • misfit_gradient gives exact gradients with it (the injection acts as sources independent of the model inside the box).

Requirements: the model must be horizontally layered, elastic and without water along the box boundary and below it (the background; it is read from the model), and the box must keep 4 cells from the absorbing layers. Inside the box the model can be anything (also attenuating). The particle velocity of the incoming wave is amplitude * stf(t) (for source time functions with integral 1, such as the default :kupper, it carries the displacement amplitude [m]), it reaches the box first at t0, and backazimuth is the direction towards the source (clockwise from north, +y); in 2D 270° (from −x, the default) or 90°. SH waves are polarized 90° counterclockwise from the direction of propagation (seen from above).

Accuracy (100 and 200 m grids, see Validation): seismograms agree with the analytical solution of a half-space (P and SV, the wave and its reflections at the free surface) to 0.1–0.5 %, with the exact solution for a crust over a mantle (also oblique to the grid in 3D) to 0.1–0.6 %; the ratio of horizontal and vertical motion at the surface is exact to 2·10⁻⁴ (P at 0–40°), and waves leaking out of the box stay below 10⁻³ of the incoming wave. An example: teleseismic_benchmark.jl.

PlaneWave (OpenSWPC) instead starts the wave as an initial wavefield that has to fit into the model: at oblique incidence it is cut where it reaches the sides, and the edges of the model send spurious surface waves of a few percent of its amplitude.

Between grid points

By default (interpolation = :cell), a source or station is placed at the cell that contains it, as in OpenSWPC: the position is rounded to the grid. With simulate(...; interpolation=:sinc) sources and stations sit at their exact position: every quantity is spread onto (sources) or interpolated from (stations) the points of its own staggered grid around it with a Kaiser-windowed sinc function (Hicks, 2002; 8 points per axis, as in SeismicWaves.jl).

  • Shifting sources and stations by a fraction of a cell then shifts the seismograms smoothly; the source-location gradient of misfit_gradient is exact.
  • Points in the air (and, for shear stresses, in water) are left out and the other weights scaled up, so stations on the free surface (surface = true) and sources in water work.
  • Velocity, displacement and stress are interpolated; the strain is still taken at the cell.
  • It works with MPI (the points may lie on several processes) and on GPUs.

Source time functions

The keyword stf of simulate sets the moment-rate function (or force history) of all sources, with their origin time t0 and rise time trise (see moment_rate); a source given its own stf (e.g. MomentTensorSource(...; stf=SampledSTF(t, values)), subfaults with different slip-rate functions) uses that one:

  • :kupper (default), :boxcar, :triangle, :herrmann, :cosine, :texp (as in OpenSWPC), :ricker, :gaussian, :gaussian_derivative;
  • SOFI's wavelets: :fuchs_mueller (Fuchs-Müller), :sin3 (= :kupper), and with their parameters BerlageSTF(; n, α, φ0) and KlauderSTF(; fmin, fmax, tsweep) (the autocorrelation of a vibroseis sweep);
  • :step (a unit step at t0, e.g. a static load switched on) and :delta (a unit impulse at t0: one time step of height 1/dt; for moment tensors a step of the moment);
  • any function stf(t, t0, trise);
  • sampled values: stf = SampledSTF(t, values) (times after t0, linearly interpolated), e.g. a source time function from a source inversion, or one that is being inverted for: misfit_gradient returns the gradient with respect to it.

Stations

Station(name, x, y, z) or Station(name, lon, lat, z, proj); with surface = true the station is placed in the first solid cell below the free surface. Velocity, displacement, stress and strain are recorded every record_every steps.

Reciprocal Green's functions

reciprocal_green(model, receiver, points; component=:z) puts a unit force at the receiver and records the displacement gradients at many points (OpenSWPC's green_mode). seismogram(green, n, source) then gives the receiver seismogram for a source at any of the points, without a simulation per source.
